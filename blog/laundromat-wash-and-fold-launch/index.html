Laundromat Wash-and-Fold Launch 2026: $2K/MO Add-On Real Math

3 Aug 2026

An unattended laundromat maxes out at whatever revenue its walk-in customers self-generate. A store with a wash-and-fold service adds $2K–$3K/month of predictable recurring revenue on top — from customers who don’t want to spend 90 minutes doing their own laundry. For most stores, wash-and-fold is the highest-ROI service expansion available, requiring $2K–$4K in equipment + supplies + one part-time attendant, and paying back inside 4–6 months.

But it’s not free money. Wash-and-fold is a service business inside a laundromat, with its own pricing structure, workflow, quality standards, and customer expectations. Launching it wrong — bad pricing, sloppy folding, unreliable turnaround — damages the store’s brand and can cost more than the added revenue produces.

This guide is the launch playbook.

Note: Wash-and-fold pricing + operational patterns vary by market, competition, and existing store profile. The figures below are 2026 U.S. averages for typical mid-cost suburban / small-city markets. Adjust for local conditions.

Why Wash-and-Fold Is Worth Adding

Three financial reasons wash-and-fold consistently pays back:

  1. Recurring revenue base. Wash-and-fold customers cluster into weekly or bi-weekly patterns. A book of 20 recurring customers = $2,400–$3,600/month baseline before any walk-in growth.
  2. Higher margin per pound. Self-service wash generates ~$1.85 per cycle. Wash-and-fold generates $2.00–$3.00 per pound at 6–12 lb/customer visit. Effective revenue per equipment cycle is 2–3× higher.
  3. Store differentiation. In a market with 3+ nearby laundromats, wash-and-fold is often the deciding factor for busy customers choosing which store to use.

The 2026 baseline: a mid-size store adding wash-and-fold sees $18K–$32K/year additional revenue after Year 1 ramp, at a 55–68% gross margin.

2026 Wash-and-Fold Pricing

Two pricing models dominate:

Per-Pound Pricing (Most Common)

  • Basic wash-and-fold: $1.85–$2.50 per pound
  • Premium (fabric softener, special detergent): $2.50–$3.50 per pound
  • Same-day rush: +25% surcharge
  • Delicates / hand-finish: +$8–$15 per item

Typical customer visit: 8–15 pounds = $15–$50 per visit.

Bag Pricing (Simpler for Attendants)

  • Small bag (up to 10 lb): $22–$30 flat
  • Medium bag (10–20 lb): $40–$55 flat
  • Large bag (20–35 lb): $65–$95 flat

Bag pricing is easier for attendants to explain + faster at the counter. Per-pound is more accurate for the customer.

Most stores start with per-pound and shift to bag pricing after 6 months if margins are consistent.

The Intake + Turnaround Workflow

The workflow that consistently produces satisfied customers:

Step 1 — Intake (60–90 seconds per customer):

  • Weigh the bag on scale
  • Fill out intake tag (name, phone, weight, special instructions)
  • Assign intake number + attach tag to bag
  • Text or email confirmation to customer
  • Confirm pickup time (standard 24-hour turnaround)

Step 2 — Wash + Dry (2–3 hours):

  • Sort by color + fabric (attendant discretion or per intake note)
  • Wash on customer’s preferred cycle (default: warm + normal)
  • Dry to full completion (never damp)

Step 3 — Fold + Package (30–45 min per bag):

  • Fold neatly to a consistent standard (train once, enforce always)
  • Package in customer’s original bag or store-branded bag
  • Hang shirts on wire hangers if requested
  • Attach return tag with pickup time

Step 4 — Pickup:

  • Verify intake number + customer ID
  • Process payment (POS card or app)
  • Hand over neatly stacked bag

Total attendant time per typical 12-lb bag: 40–50 minutes across the workflow (intake + fold + package). At $18/hour attendant wage, labor cost per bag: ~$14. Revenue per bag at $2.35/lb: $28. Gross margin per bag: ~50%.

Staffing the Service

Two staffing models:

Model 1: Existing attendant + wash-fold (best for stores already attended)

  • Add wash-fold to existing attendant duties
  • Set specific “wash-fold acceptance hours” (e.g. Mon–Sat 10am–4pm)
  • Marginal labor cost: $0 (existing attendant just takes on additional work)

Model 2: Dedicated wash-fold attendant (best for unattended stores adding service)

  • Part-time 20 hr/week attendant, $16–$22/hour
  • Weekly labor cost: $320–$440
  • Break-even at 12+ bags/week

Most stores start with Model 1 to test demand, then move to Model 2 as volume grows.

First 30 Customers — Launch Playbook

Week 1: Soft launch

  • Signage in-store announcing service
  • Attendant offers wash-fold to every 3rd walk-in customer
  • Target: 3–5 first-time users

Week 2: Local awareness

  • Update Google Business Profile with wash-and-fold service tag
  • Post in local Facebook groups: “New service at [store name] — drop off, pick up next day”
  • Nextdoor announcement in surrounding neighborhoods
  • Target: 8–12 total users

Week 3: Apartment building outreach

  • Property manager visits at 3 nearby apartment buildings
  • Leave 100 flyers per building
  • Offer 15% discount for the building’s first 20 sign-ups
  • Target: 15–20 total users

Week 4: Referral engagement

  • Every completed order gets: “Refer a friend and both get $5 off next order”
  • Ask for Google review
  • Target: 25–30 total users

By Day 30: 20–30 recurring customers, ~$500–$900/week revenue baseline. Month 3–4 typically reaches $2K/mo run rate.

Common Wash-and-Fold Mistakes

Three that damage margin + brand:

  1. Underpricing to “get started.” Setting $1.25/lb because “we need customers” trains the market permanently. Price at market from Day 1.
  2. Inconsistent folding standards. Customer #1 gets neatly folded shirts; customer #12 gets crumpled t-shirts. Kills retention. Train one standard, enforce always.
  3. Skipping the intake form. Verbal-only intake creates disputes over special instructions, timing, and payment. Written intake tag protects everyone.

Growth: Wash-and-Fold to Pickup + Delivery

After Year 1 with a stable walk-in wash-fold base, the natural expansion is pickup + delivery:

  • Adds $12K–$25K vehicle cost
  • Subscription pricing model ($40–$70/month for weekly pickup)
  • Reaches customers within 5-mile radius who never enter the store
  • Adds $3K–$8K/month revenue at Year 2+ maturity

Not right for Year 1 launch. Right for Year 2–3 as walk-in wash-fold demand plateaus.

FAQ

How much does it cost to launch wash-and-fold? Under $2,000 in equipment + supplies for walk-in only. Pickup + delivery adds $12K–$25K for vehicle.

How much revenue can wash-and-fold add? $2,000–$3,600/month recurring after Year 1 ramp for a typical mid-size store. Higher for stores with strong apartment density.

How long until wash-and-fold pays back the setup cost? 4–6 months for walk-in-only launch. Longer for pickup/delivery expansion.

Do I need a separate license for wash-and-fold? Usually no — wash-and-fold is part of the laundromat’s existing service license. Some states require a separate professional services license. Verify with your state.

Can I offer wash-and-fold at an unattended store? Yes, but only with a dedicated part-time attendant during specific acceptance hours. Fully unattended wash-and-fold doesn’t work — the intake + folding needs a person.
